# Tomato profile research dossier

> Status: first broad-source pass, still open. This dossier is not an assertion that every tomato compound or source has been found. Only claims supported for their stated sample context belong on the site. The public profile is raw grape tomatoes (USDA FDC 321360); most study leads below use other cultivars, broader tomato classes, or tissue-specific experimental samples.

## Food identity

- Common food name: Tomatoes, grape, raw
- Accepted taxon: Solanum lycopersicum
- Food form: raw, whole grape tomato; USDA Foundation Foods record
- Cultivar of FDC sample: not specified in the current profile data
- Food Data Central ID: 321360
- Current source record: https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/food-details/321360/nutrients
- Dossier opened: 2026-10-14
- Scope: raw and uncooked food evidence only. Results from cooked products are excluded from this profile.

## Current public claims and boundaries

- USDA-linked food-level entries currently show lycopene, vitamin C, and dietary fiber with their recorded measurement context.
- The carbohydrate map adds qualitative literature context for free glucose, fructose, sucrose, pectin, cellulose, and xyloglucan. It shows no amount for these records because the study samples do not match this FDC sample.
- Galactose and arabinose are described only as pectin side-chain residues in the cited structural context; they are not claimed as free sugars in this sample.
- Tomato proteomic datasets exist, but protein identities have not yet been curated into the public food record. The protein filter should guide a researcher toward these datasets and mark the task as open.
- Whole-food absence is never inferred from the current short list.

## Research reliability and open tasks

- Current source coverage: useful initial cross-source set; not exhaustive.
- Source identities: several literature records use specific cultivars or experimental populations rather than retail grape tomatoes.
- Protein data: available repository leads, but no food-record protein accession list reviewed yet.
- Metabolomics identification: prioritize authentic standards/MS-MS support; keep putative annotations separate.
- Exact per-gram amounts: add only when the actual raw food, tissue, basis, and method fit the claim.
- Next high-value contribution: review USDA sample provenance, then curate one metabolomics or proteomics dataset into separate tissue-specific records with raw sample details and reliability notes.
